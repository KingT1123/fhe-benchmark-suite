\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Stated plainly rather than left implicit:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{$N{=}2048$, category 5 sits at the edge of feasibility.} It is included in the grid and in every measured scenario, but only with a single 26-bit prime (Table~\ref{tab:paramgrid}) and a reduced plaintext modulus ($t=12289$). Its BFV/BGV fresh noise margin is about 1 bit, so it supports no multiplicative depth; its correctness was stability-tested separately (Section~\ref{sec:security-validation}).
  \item \textbf{Matrix--vector multiplication is out of scope for this stage.} It was removed from the planned composite workloads with the supervisor's agreement and remains a possible extension. The composite workloads that remain in scope are the dot product and polynomial evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Energy is not isolated to the benchmark process.} RAPL measures the whole processor package during the measurement window, not one process's share of it. Background load was minimized but not eliminated.
  \item \textbf{Net energy depends on an idle baseline that is uncertain on this laptop.} Idle power drifts between about 9 and 14\,W and per-cell readings are unreliable while the processor is hot, so net energy uses one pooled baseline (13.5\,W) and carries the sensitivity given in Section~\ref{sec:energy-protocol}; gross energy is reported alongside because it needs no such assumption. The laptop ran at about 88--90\,$^\circ$C on average during these runs.
  \item \textbf{Only Standard and Constrained use the dedicated energy/memory protocol.} Edge/Batch, Composite and Packing energy and memory values come from the earlier protocol, which measures a whole program run (start-up, input preparation, and all repetitions) and polls memory from outside; they are not comparable to Table~\ref{tab:energy-memory} and are not used for conclusions about the cost of single operations.
  \item \textbf{No DRAM-domain energy is available on this hardware.} Only \texttt{package-0} and a \texttt{core} subzone are exposed under \texttt{/sys/class/powercap/}; package energy is the value reported throughout this report.
  \item \textbf{Apparent per-item energy improvement at larger batch sizes} (Section~\ref{sec:results}) is attributed to fixed Docker invocation overhead amortizing over more work, not a genuine drop in per-operation energy cost -- consistent with the flat per-item latency result.
  \item \textbf{The Constrained (``Resource-Constrained x86'') scenario reproduces a resource ceiling, not an architecture.} No physical ARM device was available; the 4-core/4\,GB cap was applied to the same x86-64 workstation via Docker, with the specific cap values drawn from Rahman et al.~\cite{rahman2024}'s Raspberry Pi 4 profile. This tests sensitivity to limited resources, not genuine cross-architecture behavior.
  \item \textbf{Testing at $N=16384$ was limited to category~1 of the Standard scenario.} This is the largest and most resource-intensive configuration in the grid; sustained multi-hour runs at this size produced thermal instability on the development workstation, including an unrelated display fault that required investigation before testing could safely resume. On these safety grounds, categories 3 and 5 at $N=16384$ for the Standard scenario, and $N=16384$ entirely for the Constrained and Edge/Batch scenarios and the noise-budget and CKKS-error measurements, were excluded from this evaluation and instead run only up to $N=8192$ (storage was measured at $N=16384$/category~1).
\end{itemize}
